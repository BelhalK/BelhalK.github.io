    Before you build
    • Width: 2 ft is easy to reach all the way across from one side, so it suits a bed against a wall, fence or pergola post. It holds about 40% less soil, so it's lighter but dries out faster. 3 ft gives half again as much growing space, but you need to reach it from both sides.
    • Height: 30–32″ is about kitchen-counter height, so you garden standing up with no bending. 36″ suits taller people. Around 30″ also works from a chair or wheelchair.
    • Weight: wet soil weighs about 100 lb per cu ft, so a full 3×8 bed is roughly a ton. Build it where it will stay, on level ground, pavers or a patio, and don't skip the middle legs.
    • Depth: ~10″ of soil is plenty for flowers, herbs, lettuce, strawberries and other shallow roots. It isn't enough for carrots, potatoes or big tomatoes.
    • Water: elevated soil dries out faster than a bed on the ground. A drip line or a soaker hose on a timer helps. The slat gaps and fabric let water drain, so set it where drips won't stain the patio.
    • Sun: most vegetables want 6+ hours of direct sun. Pergola slats give dappled shade, which suits lettuce, herbs, greens and many flowers.
    • Wood: cedar looks best and lasts. Douglas fir or modern pressure-treated lumber costs about half as much and is generally considered fine for edible beds.
    • Tools: circular saw (or have the store make the cuts), drill/driver, tape measure, speed square, level, and a couple of clamps.
